What is emergency potable water?

Emergency potable water is drinking-quality water supplied temporarily when the normal supply has been interrupted or is unavailable. For a large site, this can involve tanker-delivered potable water being transferred into suitable receiving storage or an agreed site connection.

Potable water is different from general-purpose bulk water. Where the water is intended for drinking, cooking, hand washing or other human-consumption purposes, the supply and delivery arrangement needs to be appropriate for potable use.

The Drinking Water Inspectorate recognises emergency tanker supply as one possible temporary arrangement where a normal drinking-water supply is disrupted.

Gatwick water tanker delivery: what information is needed?

Emergency water delivery works much better when the supplier has accurate information from the start. A tanker cannot simply be sent to “Gatwick” without knowing where it needs to go, how much water is required and how the water will be received.

Exact site location

Provide the full site address and postcode wherever possible. For Gatwick Airport itself, identify the terminal, building, service area, contractor compound or operational location involved.

Access requirements

Airport deliveries may involve controlled access, security procedures, designated routes and timed deliveries. If the site has specific access arrangements, these should be confirmed by the authorised site contact.

Water quantity

Give the best estimate you have of the required volume. The quantity may depend on the number of people affected, the facilities that have lost supply, available storage and how long the outage is expected to last.

Receiving storage

Identify where the tanker will discharge. This could be a suitable storage tank or another approved receiving arrangement. The distance between the tanker and receiving point, hose route and connection details may all matter.

Delivery timing

Explain when the water is required and whether the requirement is urgent. If the outage is expected to continue, say so. Repeat tanker deliveries may be more practical than waiting until storage is almost empty.

Why tanker access matters at Gatwick

Airport environments can have restrictions that do not apply to ordinary commercial sites. Vehicle movements, security, traffic management, delivery windows and operational activity can all influence where a tanker can go.

For that reason, the delivery point should be discussed before the tanker arrives. The best tanker in the world is not much use if the vehicle cannot reach the receiving point or the water cannot be transferred safely.

If a delivery is within a controlled airport area, the site's own authorised team should establish and communicate the relevant access arrangements. P&S can then work from the practical delivery information provided.

How much emergency water does Gatwick need?

There is no single answer. A small hotel, contractor compound and major airport facility will have very different requirements.

The calculation should consider the number of people affected, the purpose of the water, the available storage and the expected duration of the interruption. Drinking and domestic requirements may be different from other operational requirements, so the intended use should be made clear.

If the requirement is uncertain, provide the best estimate available. It is much easier to refine an enquiry when the site has already established the approximate scale of the problem.

Can emergency water be delivered more than once?

Yes. If a water interruption continues, repeat deliveries may be required. This is particularly relevant to hotels, large commercial premises, construction compounds and high-footfall sites where consumption can continue throughout the day.

Repeat deliveries should be planned around the storage available on site. The aim is to avoid reaching the point where the remaining water is almost exhausted before the next delivery has been arranged.
