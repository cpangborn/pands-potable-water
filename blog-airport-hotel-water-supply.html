Why airports, hotels and large commercial premises need a practical plan for temporary or emergency drinking-water supply.

Airport water supply · Hotel water supply · Emergency tanker delivery

Airports and hotels have one thing in common when it comes to water: an interruption can affect a large number of people very quickly. A temporary potable water tanker delivery can help provide an alternative supply while the normal arrangement is being restored.

Hotels near Gatwick and other airports

Hotels close to major airports can have high occupancy, busy service areas and time-sensitive operations. A loss of water can affect guest rooms, kitchens, staff facilities and cleaning operations, making early planning important.

Airport and aviation sites

Airport-related sites can have controlled access, security requirements and operational constraints. A delivery plan should therefore consider where the tanker can enter, where it can position and how the water can reach the receiving storage.

Temporary water supply can be planned

Not every requirement is an emergency. Planned maintenance, refurbishment, temporary accommodation or changes to a site's water system can create a need for scheduled potable water deliveries.

For airport-area deliveriesGive us the exact site location, postcode, required time, approximate volume and any access or security information that could affect a tanker delivery.

Why potable water matters

Water intended for drinking, cooking and domestic uses has specific quality requirements. The Drinking Water Inspectorate's guidance covers water supplied by tankers and recognises emergency tanker supply as a possible temporary response when a normal supply problem occurs.

Whether you operate a hotel near Gatwick, an airport-related facility, a construction project or another commercial site, P&S can discuss the practical requirements for tanker-delivered potable water.
