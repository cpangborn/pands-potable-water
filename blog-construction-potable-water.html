Construction sites · Welfare facilities · Potable water tankers

Construction projects can need potable water before permanent services are available, while temporary site arrangements are being changed or during planned works. A tanker delivery can provide a practical temporary supply when the site needs drinking water on a defined schedule.

What does a construction site need to tell the tanker supplier?

The most useful information is the site address, postcode, required date, approximate quantity, vehicle access and where the water will be stored or discharged.

Consider the welfare facilities

Site welfare arrangements can include drinking water, kitchens, washing and sanitary facilities. The required quantity depends on the number of people using the site and how long the temporary arrangement will remain in place.

Plan delivery access early

Construction sites change constantly. Temporary roads, hoarding, plant movements and restricted access can affect where a tanker can safely position. Confirming the route and discharge point before delivery reduces avoidable problems.

Planning a recurring construction water supply?Tell P&S how long the project is expected to run and the approximate usage. Planned tanker deliveries can then be discussed around the site's programme.

Emergency construction water

If a site unexpectedly loses its normal potable water supply, an emergency delivery may be required. In that situation, contact the supplier as early as possible with the location, quantity and access information you have available.
