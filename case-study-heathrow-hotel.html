CASE STUDY · HOTEL WATER SUPPLY

Emergency potable water delivery
for a Heathrow-area hotel.

How an urgent hotel water-supply requirement can be approached when the normal supply is unavailable and the site still needs to operate.

THE SCENARIO

A hotel without its normal water supply.

This anonymised case study is based on a typical emergency potable water requirement and is intended to demonstrate the planning process rather than identify a particular hotel or claim a specific historic delivery.

A hotel in the Heathrow Airport area experiences an unexpected interruption to its normal water supply. With guests in the building and normal hotel operations continuing, the facilities team needs an alternative source of potable water while the underlying issue is being dealt with.

For a hotel, losing water is not simply an inconvenience. Drinking water, kitchens, guest bathrooms, staff facilities and other domestic uses can all be affected. The priority becomes establishing what is needed, where the water can be received and how a tanker delivery can be carried out safely.

THE RESPONSE

Four practical questions
come first.

01

Where is the hotel?

The exact location and delivery access need to be established, particularly around a busy airport environment where vehicle access, security and timing can matter.

02

How much water is required?

The requirement needs to be estimated from the number of people on site, the affected facilities, available storage and the expected duration of the interruption.

03

Where can the tanker discharge?

The receiving point, hose route, storage arrangements and access need to be understood before the vehicle arrives.

WHY PLANNING MATTERS

An airport-area hotel is a different delivery environment.

Hotels close to Heathrow can have high occupancy, busy service areas and restricted vehicle movements. A water tanker delivery therefore needs to be planned around the site rather than treated as a simple drop-off.

The practical details can include delivery timing, vehicle access, reversing arrangements, the distance between the tanker and storage point, hose routing and who will receive the delivery on site.

The Drinking Water Inspectorate recognises emergency tanker supply as a possible temporary response when a water supply problem creates a need for alternative water. Water supplied for human consumption must meet the applicable requirements. citeturn0search1

THE RESULT

A temporary supply while the underlying problem is resolved.

The objective of an emergency tanker delivery is not to replace the hotel's permanent water system. It is to provide a practical temporary supply that helps maintain essential operations while the normal supply is being restored.

Once the immediate requirement is understood, the delivery can be planned around the available storage and expected consumption. If the interruption continues, further deliveries may need to be scheduled rather than waiting until the hotel runs out again.

For hotels, airports and other high-footfall premises, early communication is valuable. Having the location, estimated volume, storage arrangements and access details ready can make an urgent potable water enquiry much easier to deal with.
